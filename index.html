02 · Proposed strategy · Performance marketing

Linen Sol — Winter Launch Strategy

A performance marketing approach to a new season.

A proposed strategy for the launch of Linen Sol, a new winter house brand associated with Bareeze, prepared for a Performance Marketing Specialist application. It has not been executed and contains no campaign results.

The customer journey

Five stages, one continuous journey.

01

Discover

Get in front of the right people and make the name register.

02

Explore

Turn a scroll-past into a click.

03

Trust

Remove doubt about fit, fabric, price and legitimacy.

04

Purchase

Make checkout easy and retarget near-buyers.

05

Return

Bring early customers back for the next drop.

Creative testing

Four angles worth testing first.

Need
Credibility for a new brand.
Test
A short brand-story video introducing Linen Sol as Bareeze’s winter house.
Measure
Watch-through, reach, brand-name search lift.
Need
Pieces that are warm but still stylish.
Test
A styling reel showing one piece worn three ways.
Measure
CTR, saves and shares, add-to-cart rate.
Need
Picturing a piece on your own body.
Test
UGC-style content and product pages with more than one body type.
Measure
Time on page, add-to-cart rate, checkout drop-off.
Need
Early-access urgency for a new drop.
Test
Countdown-style ads for the first two weeks.
Measure
CTR, conversion rate, and whether urgency lowers CAC.

Audience testing

Testing who we talk to, not assuming it.

  • ColdNew audiences

    Reached through fashion, winter-wear and Bareeze-adjacent interests.

  • WarmEngaged audiences

    People engaged with fashion content, or with Linen Sol itself.

  • HotVisitors and cart abandoners

    Retargeted with the pieces they actually looked at.

  • LoyalExisting customers

    Once there are any: new arrivals and early access.

Small, deliberate tests first, then budget moves to whatever responds.

Website conversion

The page has to do what a fitting room does.

Petite
Average
Tall

Show the same outfit on more than one body and height.

A shopper can’t picture a piece on herself from a single model. A small range of heights and body shapes may help, and AI-assisted visualisation could extend a look without extra shoots.

A hypothesis to A/B test on a few product pages first.

  • Time on page
  • Add-to-cart rate
  • Checkout drop-off

Also on the page

  • Product information
  • Size guidance
  • Fabric details
  • Delivery & returns
  • Photography
  • Social proof

Measure → Learn → Improve → Scale

The numbers I’d watch.

  • CTR
  • CPC
  • Add-to-cart rate
  • Conversion rate
  • CAC
  • ROAS
  • Revenue

Stop what can’t earn attention, fix what loses people before checkout, change one variable at a time, and scale what proves itself gradually.

03 · Independent concept · Brand positioning

Wear Your Version

Positioning for the modern Pakistani woman.

One woman, many versions

at work.at home.with friends.on her own.
01

The challenge

Pakistani fashion brands look alike, and a good idea gets copied within weeks.

02

The insight

When the clothes are equally good, people pick the brand that matches who they want to be.

03

The positioning

The wardrobe for every version of a woman, not a campaign for one season.

The competitive context · who owns which idea

  • KhaadiCultural identity
  • SapphireModern elegance
  • GenerationContemporary
  • LimelightAccessibility
  • ZellburyEveryday value
  • BeechtreePersonal expression: the open territory
Proposed concept · not commissioned or used by Beechtree

Creative direction

One idea, told through a day.

01

The consumer insight

People choose the brand that matches who they want to be. A woman is not one person all day: she moves between roles and wants to feel like herself in each.

02

The platform

“Version” is the idea that keeps coming back, shown through the different roles a woman moves through in a day.

03

What it makes Beechtree

A brand about personal expression, a space no competitor has claimed, instead of one more seasonal campaign.

A proposed concept of my own. Beechtree did not commission it and has not used it.

Spreadly / Cocoavive

“Why settle for the smooth?”

A premium, honest-label chocolate spread, taken from research to a live online store.

My researchFocus groups and product research, all done by me.
The insightTaste first, then trust.
The buildIdentity, two products, live store.
Visit the live site ↗Read the case
500g jar · Rs 2,900250g pouch · Rs 1,850
HoneyDate syrupChiaKalonji

The case

Taste first, then trust.

01

The problem

Chocolate spread in Pakistan is either an imported jar full of refined sugar or a cheap local one people don’t fully trust. Nothing sits in between.

02

The insight

People buy on taste before health. Honey, dates and kalonji are already trusted in Pakistani kitchens, which makes a healthier spread believable.

03

What we built

An identity under the line “Why settle for the smooth?”, two products, a blog and a live e-commerce store.

04

Ingredients

Honey, date syrup, chia seeds and kalonji.

05

What I did on my own

I ran the focus groups and the product research entirely by myself.

06

What we did together

The brand and the store were built with one teammate as a two-person project.

A proof of concept that was never launched commercially, so no sales are claimed.
